### The Alphabet and the Light: The Estrangela Script
---

#### Section 1: Introduction to the Script

Syriac is written from **right to left**. The script used in this course is **Estrangela** (ܐܣܛܪܢܓܠܐ), meaning "rounded." It is the oldest and most formal of the three Syriac scripts, and it is the primary script used in the earliest manuscripts of the Peshitta and the Acts of the Martyrs.

**Core Principles:**
1. **22 Consonants**: All letters are consonants. Vowels are indicated by separate small marks or dots (to be covered in Lesson 1).
2. **Connecting Letters**: Most letters connect to the preceding and following letters (like cursive or Arabic). However, 8 letters are "non-joiners" — they only connect to the letter before them.
3. **No Case**: There are no capital letters.
4. **Alaph as Carrier**: The first letter, Alaph (ܐ), is often a silent carrier for a vowel or a glottal stop.

---

#### Section 2: The 22 Letters (The Abgad System)

Syriac letters follow the same order as Hebrew and the ancient "Abgad" order of Arabic.

#### Section 3: Joiners vs. Non-Joiners

In your reading, you will notice that certain letters never connect to the letter following them. This creates a small visual gap within a word.

**The Non-Joiners (Letters that only connect to the RIGHT):**
ܐ (Alaph), ܕ (Dalath), ܗ (He), ܘ (Waw), ܙ (Zain), ܨ (Sade), ܪ (Rish), ܬ (Taw).

*Note: Taw (ܬ) often appears to connect, but technically follows the non-joiner rule in many formal manuscripts. Dalath (ܕ) and Rish (ܪ) look identical except for a single dot: Dalath has a dot BELOW, Rish has a dot ABOVE.*
