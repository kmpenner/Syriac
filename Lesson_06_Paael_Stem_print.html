Section 2: The Paael Stem

Core Meaning: Intensive and Factitive

The Paael stem (also spelled Pael or Paʿʿel, ܦܥܠ) expresses two related types of intensified meaning:

  • Intensive: Repeated, thoroughgoing, or heightened action. “He broke” (Peal tbar) → “he shattered into pieces” (Paael tabbar).
  • Factitive: Causing someone or something to enter into a state. “Be holy” (Peal qdaš) → “make holy / sanctify” (Paael qaddeš); “learn” (Peal īlep) → “make learned / teach” (Paael ʾallep). The Paael turns intransitive or stative verbs into transitive ones.

Formation: Gemination of the Middle Root Letter

The defining feature of the Paael is the doubling (gemination) of the middle root consonant (R2).

  • Perfect: R₁aR₂R₂eR₃ pattern — e.g., barrek (from b-r-k), qaddeš (from q-d-š), ʾallep (from ʾ-l-p / y-l-p, “teach”).
  • Participle: mR₁aR₂R₂eR₃ pattern (m- prefix) — e.g., mbarrek, mqaddeš, mallep (where the initial ʾ of ʾallep elides in spelling: ܡܠܦ).

Cross-Linguistic Comparison

  • Hebrew Piel: The direct cognate of the Syriac Paael. Also formed by doubling R2. Compare: kipper (Piel, “atone/cover” — from k-p-r), bēraḵ (Piel, “bless” — from b-r-k), qiddēš (Piel, “sanctify”). The structural identity is near-perfect.
  • Arabic Form II: Formed by doubling R2 (qaddama, “to present/offer”; ʿallama, “to teach/instruct”; qaddasa, “to sanctify”). Arabic Form II corresponds precisely to Syriac Paael in both form and function.
  • Greek: No direct morphological equivalent. Intensives expressed via prefix verbs (e.g., κατα- compounds), aspect, or adverbs. Latin uses intensifying prefixes or separate lexical items.

Paael Examples: Peal → Paael

Peal (Syr) Root Peal Meaning Paael (Syr) Paael Translit Paael Meaning
ܒܪܟ b-r-k kneel (Peal brak) ܒܪܟ barrek bless, pronounce blessing
ܩܕܫ q-d-š be holy (Peal qdaš) ܩܕܫ qaddeš sanctify, make holy
ܝܠܦ y-l-p / ʾ-l-p learn (Peal īlep) ܐܠܦ ʾallep teach (make learned)
ܫܡܫ š-m-š (used in Paael) ܫܡܫ šammeš minister, serve formally
ܟܢܫ k-n-š gather (intrans., Peal knaš) ܟܢܫ kanneš assemble, collect (trans.)

KEY INSIGHT: When the Paael is applied to an INTRANSITIVE or STATIVE verb (e.g., “be holy” or “learn”), it makes the action TRANSITIVE and FACTITIVE: “make X holy / sanctify” (qaddeš) or “make X learn / teach” (ʾallep). Note that “teach” in Syriac is a I-Alaph Paael verb: perfect ܐܠܦ (ʾallep) and participle ܡܠܦ (mallep).

Formation Notes

  • Root: b-r-k. In the Paael, R2 (r) is geminated in the morphological pattern: barrek (compare qaddeš, šabbeḥ, mallel).
  • Perfect vowel pattern: a under R1, doubled R2, e vowel between R2 and R3 (elided before vocalic endings -at and -et: barrkat, barrket).
  • Suffix set: Identical to Peal and Aphel perfect suffixes.
  • I-Alaph root ʾallep (“to teach”): Conjugates regularly on this pattern: 3 m.sg. ܐܠܦ (ʾallep), 3 f.sg. ܐܠܦܬ (ʾallpat), 2 m.sg. ܐܠܦܬ (ʾallept), 1 c.sg. ܐܠܦܬ (ʾallpet), 3 m.pl. ܐܠܦܘ (ʾallepū), 1 c.pl. ܐܠܦܢ (ʾallepn).

Section 4: Paael Participle Paradigm

Root: b-r-k | Stem: Paael | Meaning: “blessing”

The Paael active participle takes the same m- prefix as all derived-stem participles. The gemination of R2 is preserved throughout.

Form Estrangela Transliteration Translation
m.sg. ܡܒܪܟ mbarrek blessing (m.sg.)
f.sg. ܡܒܪܟܐ mbarrkā blessing (f.sg.)
m.pl. ܡܒܪܟܝܢ mbarrkīn blessing (m.pl.)
f.pl. ܡܒܪܟܢ mbarrkān blessing (f.pl.)

Formation Notes

  • Prefix: m- (identical to Aphel participle prefix).
  • Vowel pattern: m + R1 + a + R2R2 + e + R3 (preserves gemination of R2). For ܐܠܦ (ʾallep, “teach”), the initial Alaph elides after m-: m.sg. ܡܠܦ (mallep), f.sg. ܡܠܦܐ (mallpā), m.pl. ܡܠܦܝܢ (mallpīn), f.pl. ܡܠܦܢ (mallpān).
  • Suffixes: Standard participle endings: m.sg. (unmarked), f.sg. (-ā), m.pl. (-īn), f.pl. (-ān).

Comparison with Hebrew and Arabic

  • Hebrew Piel participle: prefix מְ (mĕ-) + doubled R2. E.g., mĕbārēḵ (blessing, from b-r-k Piel), mĕqaddēš (sanctifying, from q-d-š Piel). Structurally identical to Syriac Paael participle.
  • Arabic Form II active participle: pattern muCaCCiC (mu + R1 + a + R2R2 + i + R3). E.g., muʿallim (teacher, from ʿ-l-m Form II), muqaddis (sanctifier, from q-d-s Form II). The m- prefix and gemination are pan-Semitic features.

Section 6: The Story — Part 6

Yukhannan Blesses the People and Angers the Priests

Narrative note: This passage is rich in Paael forms (barrek, ṣallī, šabbeḥ, ʾallep, mqaddeš, mšammeš). It also introduces the central conflict of the Acts: Yukhannan’s ministry challenges the established religious order. Notice the theological contrast between sacrifice (qūrbānā) and faith (haymānūtā).

Key Paael forms: barrek (blessed), ṣallī (prayed), šabbeḥ (praised), ʾallep (taught), mqaddeš (sanctifies, Paael participle), mšammeš (ministering). Note also metqaddaš in sentence 9 — the Etpaael participle (passive of Paael, “is sanctified”), previewing Lesson 10.

Sentence 1

ܘܝܘܚܢܢ ܩܡ ܥܠ ܡܕܒܚܐ ܘܒܪܟ ܠܥܡܐ

w-Yūḥannan qām ʿal madbḥā w-barrek l-ʿammā

w- (and-) Yūḥannan (Yukhannan) qām (stood) ʿal (by/upon) madbḥā (the-altar) w-barrek (and-blessed) l-ʿammā (the-people)

→ And Yukhannan stood by the altar and blessed the people.

Sentence 2

ܘܨܠܝ ܒܩܠܐ ܪܡܐ ܘܫܒܚ ܠܐܠܗܐ

w-ṣallī b-qālā rāmā w-šabbeḥ l-ʾAlāhā

w-ṣallī (and-prayed) b-qālā (with-voice) rāmā (loud) w-šabbeḥ (and-praised) l-ʾAlāhā (God)

→ He prayed with a loud voice and praised God.

Sentence 3

ܘܐܠܦ ܠܗܘܢ ܐܘܪܚܐ ܚܕܬܐ ܕܨܠܘܬܐ

w-ʾallep lhūn ʾūrḥā ḥdattā d-ṣlūtā

w-ʾallep (and-taught) lhūn (them) ʾūrḥā (the-way) ḥdattā (new) d-ṣlūtā (of-prayer)

→ He taught them the new way of prayer.

Sentence 4

ܘܐܡܪ ܠܐ ܡܕܒܚܐ ܕܟܗܢܘܬܐ ܗܘ ܕܡܩܕܫ ܐܢܫ

w-ʾemar: lā madbḥā d-kāhnūtā hū d-mqaddeš ʾnāš

w-ʾemar (and-said) lā (not) madbḥā (the-altar) d-kāhnūtā (of-the-priesthood) hū (it-is) d-mqaddeš (that-sanctifies) ʾnāš (a-person)

→ “It is not the altar of the priesthood that sanctifies a person.”

Sentence 5

ܐܠܐ ܡܪܝܐ ܗܘ ܕܡܩܕܫ ܠܟܘܢ ܒܪܘܚܗ

ʾellā Māryā hū d-mqaddeš lḵūn b-rūḥeh

ʾellā (but/rather) Māryā (the-Lord) hū (he-is) d-mqaddeš (who-sanctifies) lḵūn (you [pl.]) b-rūḥeh (by-his-Spirit)

→ “Rather, it is the Lord who sanctifies you by his Spirit.”

Sentence 6

ܫܡܥܘ ܕܝܢ ܟܗܢܐ ܡܠܘܗܝ ܘܪܓܙܘ ܥܠܘܗܝ

šmaʿū dēn kāhnē mellāw-hy w-rgezū ʿlāw-hy

šmaʿū (heard) dēn (but/now) kāhnē (the-priests) mellāw-hy (his-words) w-rgezū (and-were-angered) ʿlāw-hy (against-him)

→ But the priests heard his words and were angered against him.

Sentence 7

ܘܐܡܪܘ ܡܢ ܝܗܒ ܠܟ ܫܘܠܛܢܐ ܕܡܫܡܫ ܐܢܬ ܒܗܝܟܠܐ

w-ʾemrū: man yahab lāḵ šulṭānā d-mšammeš ʾant b-hayklā?

w-ʾemrū (and-they-said) man (who) yahab (gave) lāḵ (to-you) šulṭānā (authority) d-mšammeš (that-ministering) ʾant (you-are) b-hayklā (in-the-temple)

→ “Who gave you authority that you minister in the temple?”

Sentence 8

ܥܢܐ ܝܘܚܢܢ ܘܐܡܪ ܐܠܗܐ ܫܕܪ ܠܝ ܘܡܩܕܫ ܐܢܐ ܠܥܡܗ

ʿnā Yūḥannan w-ʾemar: ʾAlāhā šaddar lī w-mqaddeš ʾenā l-ʿammeh

ʿnā (answered) Yūḥannan (Yukhannan) w-ʾemar (and-said) ʾAlāhā (God) šaddar (sent) lī (me) w-mqaddeš (and-sanctifying) ʾenā (I-am) l-ʿammeh (his-people)

→ Yukhannan answered: “God sent me, and I am sanctifying his people.”

Sentence 9

ܠܐ ܒܩܘܪܒܢܐ ܐܠܐ ܒܗܝܡܢܘܬܐ ܡܬܩܕܫ ܐܢܫ

lā b-qūrbānā ʾellā b-haymānūtā metqaddaš ʾnāš

lā (not) b-qūrbānā (by-sacrifice) ʾellā (but) b-haymānūtā (by-faith) metqaddaš (is-sanctified) ʾnāš (a-person)

→ “A person is not sanctified by sacrifice but by faith.”

Sentence 10

ܘܪܓܙ ܪܒ ܟܗܢܐ ܘܐܦܩ ܠܗ ܡܢ ܗܝܟܠܐ

w-rgez rab kāhnē w-ʾappeq leh men hayklā

w-rgez (and-was-enraged) rab (chief) kāhnē (of-priests) w-ʾappeq (and-expelled/brought-out) leh (him) men (from) hayklā (the-temple)

→ And the chief priest was enraged and expelled him from the temple.

Sentence 11

ܝܘܚܢܢ ܕܝܢ ܠܐ ܕܚܠ ܐܠܐ ܫܒܚ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܒܟܠܗ ܠܒܗ

Yūḥannan dēn lā dḥel ʾellā šabbeḥ l-ʾAlāhā b-kulleh lebbeh

Yūḥannan (Yukhannan) dēn (but/however) lā (not) dḥel (feared) ʾellā (but) šabbeḥ (praised) l-ʾAlāhā (God) b-kulleh (with-all) lebbeh (his-heart)

→ But Yukhannan was not afraid; rather he praised God with all his heart.

Exercise 5: Peal vs. Paael Comparison (sample answers)

  1. q-d-š: Peal qdaš = intransitive “to be holy”; Paael qaddeš = factitive “to make holy, sanctify” (e.g., Kāhnā qaddeš l-madbḥā, “The priest sanctified the altar”).
  2. b-r-k: Peal brak = “to kneel”; Paael barrek = intensive/declarative “to bless” (e.g., Yūḥannan barrek l-ʿammā, “Yukhannan blessed the people”).
  3. y-l-p / ʾ-l-p: Peal īlep = “to learn” (Talmīdā īlep nāmūsā, “The disciple learned the law”); Paael ʾallep = factitive “to teach” (Yūḥannan ʾallep l-ʿammā, “Yukhannan taught the people”).
  4. š-m-š: Used in Paael šammeš = “to minister, serve formally” (Kāhnā šammeš b-hayklā, “The priest ministered in the temple”).
  5. d-k-y: Peal dkā = intransitive “to be pure, clean”; Paael dakkī = factitive “to purify, cleanse” (Dakkī mayyā, “He purified the water”).

Exercise 6: Comprehension

# Answer
1 He blessed (barrek), prayed (ṣallī), praised (šabbeḥ), and taught (ʾallep).
2 Not the altar but the Lord, by his Spirit (b-rūḥeh); a person is sanctified by faith (b-haymānūtā), not sacrifice.
3 They were angered (rgezū) — he was bypassing priestly authority.
4 Priests questioned his authority to minister in the temple; Yukhannan claimed divine commission (ʾAlāhā šaddar lī).
5 He praised God (šabbeḥ) — showing fearless devotion over fear of human authority.

FINAL REMINDER: All three active stems — Peal, Paael, and Aphel — share the same suffix set for the perfect. In the active participle, the Peal has NO prefix (kāteb), whereas the derived stems (Paael mbarrek, Aphel maqreb) take the characteristic m- prefix.
