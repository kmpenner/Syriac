Section 2: The Semitic Prefix Conjugation — Overview

We have learned that the Participle handles the Present (ongoing action) and the Perfect (Suffix Conjugation) handles the Past (completed action).

How does Syriac talk about the Future or expressing wishes/commands? It uses the Imperfect Tense, often called the “Prefix Conjugation.”

Cross-Linguistic Comparison: The Imperfect

In all Semitic languages, the Imperfect adds prefixes (n-, t-, ʾ-, y-, etc.) to the root to indicate the person, while suffixes are used to indicate plural number.

  • Hebrew Qal Imperfect: יִכְתֹּב (yiktōb — he will write), אֶכְתֹּב (ʾektōb — I will write).
  • Arabic Form I Imperfect: يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu — he writes/will write), أَكْتُبُ (ʾaktubu — I write).
  • Syriac Peal Imperfect: ܢܟܬܘܒ (nektūb — he will write), ܐܟܬܘܒ (ʾektūb — I will write).

Crucial Note on Syriac vs. Hebrew/Arabic: In Hebrew and Arabic, the 3rd person masculine prefix is y- (ya- or yi-). In standard Syriac, the 3rd person prefix shifted to n- (ne-).

Section 3: Morphology and Syntax of the Imperfect

1. Forming the Peal Imperfect

For a strong root like k-t-b, the base vocalization of the imperfect shifts to an ū (Waw) theme vowel between the second and third consonant: -ktūb. We then add the person-marking prefixes:

  • n- (He / They m) → n-ek-tūb → nektūb (He will write).
  • t- (You / She / They f) → tektūb (You will write / She will write).
  • ʾ- (I) → ʾektūb (I will write).

2. Syntax: The Simple Future

By itself, the Imperfect functions as a simple future tense.

  • Nektūb Yūḥannan meltā. (Yukhannan will write a word).
  • Teḥze tammān malkā. (You will see a king there).

3. Syntax: The Subjunctive / Purpose (d- + Imperfect)

Syriac uses the Imperfect heavily for purpose clauses, wishes, and commands. It accomplishes this by attaching the relative particle d- directly to the Imperfect verb. This corresponds to the Greek subjunctive with ἵνα (hina).

  • ʾEtā d-neḥze = He came in order that he might see.
  • ʾĀmer-nā d-tektūb = I say that you should write.

Section 4: Imperfect Tense Paradigms (Peal, Paael, Aphel)

1. The Peal Imperfect Conjugation (Root q-ṭ-l)

In the Peal, the prefix has an e vowel in modern pronunciation (or i historically), and the theme vowel is usually ū (or a for gutturals).

Person Estrangela Translit Translation Prefix Suffix
3 m.sg. ܢܩܛܘܠ neqṭūl he will kill n- (none)
3 f.sg. ܬܩܛܘܠ teqṭūl she will kill t- (none)
2 m.sg. ܬܩܛܘܠ teqṭūl you (m) will kill t- (none)
2 f.sg. ܬܩܛܠܝܢ teqṭlīn you (f) will kill t- -īn
1 c.sg. ܐܩܛܘܠ ʾeqṭūl I will kill ʾ- (none)
3 m.pl. ܢܩܛܠܘܢ neqṭlūn they (m) will kill n- -ūn
3 f.pl. ܢܩܛܠܢ neqṭlān they (f) will kill n- -ān
1 c.pl. ܢܩܛܘܠ neqṭūl we will kill n- (none)

Warning: The forms for “He will kill” (neqṭūl) and “We will kill” (neqṭūl) are identical in spelling and pronunciation! Context almost always makes it obvious.

2. The Paael Imperfect (Root q-d-š, to sanctify)

In the Paael the prefix has only a reduced vowel (nqaddeš), and the middle root letter is doubled.

Person Estrangela Translit Translation
3 m.sg. ܢܩܕܫ nqaddeš he will sanctify
1 c.sg. ܐܩܕܫ ʾqaddeš I will sanctify
3 m.pl. ܢܩܕܫܘܢ nqaddšūn they will sanctify
1 c.pl. ܢܩܕܫ nqaddeš we will sanctify

3. The Aphel Imperfect (Root n-p-q, Aphel = ʾappeq to bring out)

In the Aphel, the original ʾa- prefix of the perfect drops out, and the imperfect prefix takes its place with an a vowel. The theme vowel is e.

Person Estrangela Translit Translation
3 m.sg. ܢܦܩ nappeq he will bring out
1 c.sg. ܐܦܩ ʾappeq I will bring out
3 m.pl. ܢܦܩܘܢ nappqūn they will bring out

Section 5: The Subjunctive Flow

Observe how the purpose clause flows in translation:

Primary Action (Past/Present) Subjunctive Clause (d- + Imperfect) Translation
ʾEtā malkā (The king came) d-neqṭūl l-eh (that he might kill him) The king came in order to kill him.
Yūḥannan ʿābed nūhrā (Yukhannan makes light) d-neḥzūn ʿammā (that the people might see) Yukhannan makes light so that the people will see.

Section 7: The Story — Part 8

The Threat

Narrative note: The king uses the Subjunctive/Imperfect to express his threats and purpose concerning Yukhannan’s future.

Sentence 1

ܩܡ ܝܘܚܢܢ ܩܕܡ ܡܠܟܐ ܕܕܝܢܐ qām Yūḥannan qdām malkā d-dīnā. qām (stood) Yūḥannan (John) qdām (before) malkā (the-king) d-dīnā (of-judgment). → Yukhannan stood before the king of judgment.

Sentence 2

ܘܝܬܒ ܡܠܟܐ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗ w-īteb malkā w-ʾemar l-ēh: w-īteb (and-sat) malkā (the-king) w-ʾemar (and-said) l-ēh (to-him): → And the king sat and said to him:

Sentence 3

ܥܒܕ ܐܢܐ ܕܝܢܐ ܕܐܥܒܕ ܒܝܫܐ ܠܟ ʿābed-nā dīnā, d-ʾeʿbed bīšā lāḵ! ʿābed (doing/making) -nā (am-I) dīnā (judgment), d- (PURP) + ʾeʿbed (1sg.IMPF.PEAL of ʿ-b-d, ‘do/make’ = “in order that I might do”) bīšā (evil) lāḵ (to-you)! → “I am rendering judgment, that I might do evil to you!” Note: ʾeʿbed is a Peal imperfect 1sg in a purpose clause (introduced by d-), not a perfect or imperative. The construction d- + Imperfect corresponds to Greek ἵνα + subjunctive.

Sentence 4

ܐܢ ܠܐ ܬܫܒܘܩ ܐܪܥܝ ܢܩܛܠܘܢ ܠܟ ܒܝܘܡ ܕܝܢܐ ʾen lā tešbūq ʾarʿ-y, neqṭlūn lāḵ b-yūm dīnā! ʾen (if) lā (not) tešbūq (you-will-leave) ʾarʿ-y (land-my), neqṭlūn (they-will-kill) lāḵ (to-you) b-yūm (in-the-day) dīnā (of-judgment)! → “If you do not leave my land, they will kill you in the day of judgment!”

Sentence 5

ܝܘܚܢܢ ܐܡܪ ܠܗ ܠܐ ܐܕܚܠ Yūḥannan ʾemar l-ēh: lā ʾedḥal. Yūḥannan (John) ʾemar (said) l-ēh (to-him): lā (not) ʾedḥal (I-will-fear). → Yukhannan said to him: “I will not fear.”

Sentence 6

ܐܬܝܬ ܕܐܡܪ ܡܠܬܐ ܕܐܠܗܐ ܠܥܠܡܐ ʾetēt d-ʾemar meltā d-Alāhā l-ʿālmā. ʾetēt (I-came) d-ʾemar (that-I-might-say) meltā (the-word) d-Alāhā (of-God) l-ʿālmā (to-the-world). → “I came in order to speak the word of God to the world.”

Section 10: Grammatical Summary

THE PEAL IMPERFECT (Root q-ṭ-l)

Person Syriac Translit
3 m.sg. ܢܩܛܘܠ neqṭūl (he will kill)
3 f.sg. ܬܩܛܘܠ teqṭūl (she will kill)
2 m.sg. ܬܩܛܘܠ teqṭūl (you will kill)
1 c.sg. ܐܩܛܘܠ ʾeqṭūl (I will kill)
3 m.pl. ܢܩܛܠܘܢ neqṭlūn (they will k.)
1 c.pl. ܢܩܛܘܠ neqṭūl (we will kill)

SYNTAX OF PURPOSE

Verb 1 (Any Tense) + d- + Verb 2 (Imperfect) = Purpose Clause ʾEzal (He went) d-neʿbed (in order to do) qyāmā (a covenant).
