Section 1: Review of Previous Concepts

Quick Summary

Throughout the course, we have used the preposition l- (ܠ) to mark the direct object of a verb. For example, qṭal l-malkā (he killed the king) or qṭal l-eh (he killed him). We also learned how to attach pronominal suffixes to nouns and prepositions to indicate possession or indirect objects (bayt-eh = his house; l-eh = to him).

For a pronoun object, the construction with l- that the stories have used so far (šaddar lī, “he sent me”) was a simplification. It is good Syriac, but it is not the usual way. This lesson teaches the usual way.

Section 2: The Verbal Suffix — Overview

In Classical Syriac, the normal, unmarked way to express a pronominal direct object is to attach the object pronoun directly to the verb itself (qaṭleh, “he killed him”), whereas the analytic construction with l- (qṭal l-eh) is more marked or emphatic.

This creates a single, highly compressed word that contains the Action, the Subject, and the Direct Object.

Cross-Linguistic Comparison: Object Suffixes on Verbs

  • Hebrew: קְטָלוֹ (qṭāl-ō — he killed him). The suffix -ō is attached directly to the perfect verb.
  • Arabic: قَتَلَهُ (qatala-hu — he killed him). The suffix -hu is attached directly to the verb.
  • Syriac: ܩܛܠܗ (qaṭl-eh — he killed him). The suffix -eh attaches directly, altering the internal vowels of the verb.
  • Greek/Latin: Indo-European languages generally keep the object pronoun separate (e.g., auton apekteinen, eum interfecit), though spoken Romance languages often compress them into clitics (Spanish: matarlo).

Core Principle: A suffix attached to a noun is possessive (“his house”). A suffix attached to a verb is the direct object (“he killed him”). The suffix never represents the subject of the verb.

Section 3: Morphology of Verbal Suffixes (Peal Perfect)

When you attach a pronoun suffix to a verb, the vowels inside the verb often shift to accommodate the new rhythm. We will focus on attaching suffixes to the 3rd person masculine singular Peal perfect (qṭal, “he killed”).

1. The Object Suffixes

The object suffixes are the possessive suffixes you learned in Lesson 4, with one difference: “me” is not -(y) but -an(y), written ܢܝ. Its Yodh is silent, like the Yodh of malk(y).

  • “He killed me”: Not qṭal-y, but qaṭl-an(y) ܩܛܠܢܝ.
  • “He killed us”: qaṭl-an ܩܛܠܢ.

These two are pronounced alike (qaṭlan); the written Yodh tells them apart. After the plural endings of the imperfect and imperative an extra syllable -ān- links verb and suffix (neqṭlūnān(y) “they will kill me”, neqṭlūnāk “they will kill you”).

2. Vocalization Shifts & 3rd Plural Enclitics

When adding a light (vowel-initial) object suffix to qṭal (which has no vowel between the first two consonants: q-ṭ-a-l), the vowel shifts back to separate the first two consonants (qaṭl-). Before the heavy 2nd plural suffixes (-kūn, -kēn), the stem retains its original vocalization (qṭal-). For 3rd person plural objects (“them”), Classical Syriac does not attach -hūn or -hēn to the verb; instead, it places the enclitic pronouns ܐܢܘܢ (ʾennōn, m.pl.) and ܐܢܝܢ (ʾennēn, f.pl.) immediately after the verb.

  • qṭal + -eh → qaṭl-eh (q-a-ṭ-l-e-h, “he killed him”).
  • qṭal + ʾennōn → qṭal ʾennōn (“he killed them [m.]”).

3. Meaning

Qaṭleh means “He killed him”.

  • Subject: He (inherent in the base verb form).
  • Object: Him (the suffix).

Note: Suffixes are also attached to Imperfect and Imperative forms. On the 2 m.sg. imperative of a strong verb, the 1 c.sg. suffix takes the form -ayn(y): Peal ܦܪܘܩܝܢܝ (pruqayn(y), “save me!”). A III-Yodh imperative ends in a vowel, and the suffix attaches to it directly: Paael ܐܣܐ (ʾassā, “heal!”) gives ܐܣܢܝ (ʾassān(y), “heal me!”), just as ܚܘܐ (ḥawwā, “show!”) gives ܚܘܢܝ (ḥawwān(y), “show me!”). Compare these with the 3 m.sg. perfects ܦܪܩܢܝ (parqan(y), “he saved me”) and ܐܣܝܢܝ (ʾassyan(y), “he healed me”).

Section 7: The Story — Part 11

The Healing

Narrative note: The king, having fallen ill, begs for physical and spiritual salvation. Notice the dense use of verbal suffixes (fast direct objects) as he pleads with Yukhannan.

Sentence 1

ܘܒܝܘܡܐ ܗ̇ܘ ܢܦܠ ܡܠܟܐ ܒܟܘܪܗܢܐ ܒܝܫܐ w-b-yawmā haw npal malkā b-kūrhānā bīšā. w-b-yawmā (and-in-day) haw (that) npal (fell) malkā (the-king) b-kūrhānā (in-illness) bīšā (evil/bad). → And in that day, the king fell into a severe illness.

Sentence 2

ܘܐܬܐ ܠܘܬ ܝܘܚܢܢ ܘܢܦܠ ܩܕܡܘܗܝ ܘܐܡܪ ܠܗ w-ʾetā l-wāt Yūḥannan, w-npal qdāmaw(hy), w-ʾemar l-eh: w-ʾetā (and-came) l-wāt (to-the-presence-of) Yūḥannan (John), w-npal (and-fell) qdāmaw(hy) (before-him), w-ʾemar (and-said) l-eh (to-him): → And he came to Yukhannan, and fell before him, and said to him:

Sentence 3

ܐܣܢܝ ܡܢ ܟܘܪܗܢܝ ܘܦܪܘܩܝܢܝ ܡܢ ܡܘܬܐ ʾassān(y) men kūrhān(y), w-pruqayn(y) men mawtā! ʾassān(y) (heal-me [Paael Impv + 1csg]) men (from) kūrhān(y) (illness-my), w-pruqayn(y) (and-save-me [Peal Impv + 1csg]) men (from) mawtā (death)! → “Heal me from my illness, and save me from death!”

Sentence 4

ܘܫܒܘܩ ܠܝ ܚܛܝܬܝ ܕܣܓܝܐܐ ܗܝ w-šbūq lī ḥṭīt(y) d-saggīʾā hī! w-šbūq (and-forgive [Peal Impv]) lī (to-me) ḥṭīt(y) (sin-my) d-saggīʾā (which-is-great/much) hī (she-[is])! → “And forgive me my sin, which is great!”

Sentence 5

ܩܪܐ ܝܘܚܢܢ ܠܐܠܗܐ ܘܐܡܪ qrā Yūḥannan l-ʾAlāhā w-ʾemar: qrā (called [3msg Peal Perf. of q-r-y]) Yūḥannan (John) l-ʾAlāhā (upon-God) w-ʾemar (and-said): → Yukhannan called upon God and said: Note: Here qrā takes its object with l- (“called upon God”). With an object suffix, a III-Yodh perfect keeps its final vowel and the 3 m.sg. suffix takes the form -y(hy): ܩܪܝܗܝ qrāy(hy) “he called him”; in the plural, ܩܪܐܘܗܝ qraʾū(hy) “they called him”.

Sentence 6

ܐܠܗܐ ܦܪܘܩܝܢܝ ܗܫܐ ʾAlāhā, pruqayn(y) hāšā. ʾAlāhā (O-God), pruqayn(y) (save-me! [Peal imperative of p-r-q + 1 c.sg. suffix]) hāšā (now). → “O God, save me now!”

Sentence 7

ܘܫܡܥܗ ܐܠܗܐ ܒܛܝܒܘܬܗ ܘܫܪܝ ܠܡܐܣܝܘܬܗ w-šamʿ-eh ʾAlāhā b-ṭaybūt-eh, w-šarrī l-mʾassāyūt-eh. w-šamʿ-eh (and-heard-him) ʾAlāhā (God) b-ṭaybūt-eh (by-grace-his), w-šarrī (and-began) l-mʾassāyūt-eh (to-heal-him). → And God heard him by his grace, and began to heal him.

THE VERBAL SUFFIX PARADIGM (He killed…)

English Suffix / Enclitic on verb Example
…me -an(y) qaṭlan(y) (he killed me)
…you (m) -āk qaṭlāk (he killed you)
…you (f) -ēk(y) qaṭlēk(y) (he killed you)
…him -eh qaṭleh (he killed him)
…her -āh qaṭlāh (he killed her)
…us -an qaṭlan (he killed us)
…you (m.pl) -kūn qṭalkūn (he killed you)
…you (f.pl) -kēn qṭalkēn (he killed you)
…them (m) ʾennōn qṭal ʾennōn (he killed them)
…them (f) ʾennēn qṭal ʾennēn (he killed them)

DOUBLE DETERMINATION RULE

Syriac very often uses the verb suffix AND the preposition l- in the same sentence, the suffix anticipating a definite noun object. This is called prolepsis or double determination. It is ordinary Syriac style and usually carries no special emphasis. Qaṭl-eh l-malkā = He killed him, [namely] the king.
