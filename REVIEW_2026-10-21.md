## 1. Systematic errors (fix with a global pass, then regenerate the TSV, glossary, checks and Flash Pro deck)

1. **Abstract ending -ūtā, not -ūttā** [S]. Examples: ܡܠܟܘܬܐ is malkūṯā, ܛܝܒܘܬܐ ṭaybūṯā, ܨܠܘܬܐ ṣlūṯā (the taw has rukkākā, so it is single and spirantized). The error occurs 149 times in the lessons and appendices and in 29 TSV entries: haymānūttā, kēnūttā, maʿmūdīttā, ṣlūttā, ḥṭīttā, malkūtt-eh, and so on. Genuine doubled -tt- remains correct where n is assimilated, e.g. mdittā ܡܕܝܢܬܐ.
2. **ʾalāhā is written without ʾ** ("Alāhā"), 74 times. Every other ʾ-initial word has it.
3. **'Teach' is a Paael, not an Aphel** [S: ܐܰܠܶܦ is listed as IIa = Paael "to teach"; ܡܰܠܶܦ is the IIa participle]. The textbook gets this wrong in two contradictory ways:
   - L5 uses ʾalep / malep as the model **Aphel** paradigm. The whole Aphel lesson is therefore built on a Paael verb.
   - L6, L9 and L10 use ܝܠܦ **yallep** / **ʾetyallap** for 'teach' / 'be taught' (19 times). That form does not exist; the Paael is ܐܠܦ ʾallep (West ʾalep), and the Ethpaal is ʾetallap.

   Fix: give L5 a strong Aphel model, e.g. ʾaqreb (already used in Ex. 2 and Appendix A). Move ʾallep/mallep to L6 as an example of a I-ʾ Paael. Replace every yallep.
4. **Aphel of ḥ-y-y is ʾaḥī, not ʾaḥyī** [S: ܐܰܚܺܝ]. 17 occurrences, plus the glossary.
5. **Aphel perfect of g-l-y is spelled ܐܓܠܝ.** The textbook writes ܐܓܠܐ, which is the 1 c.sg. imperfect [S]. 6 occurrences (L5, L6 warm-up, L7 warm-up, glossary).
6. **Masculine construct singular is melek, not malk** (L1 §5, L2 warm-up, L5 key Q2, Appendix A.2). For masculine singulars the absolute and construct forms are identical. *malk-* is the stem used before suffixes, which is a different thing.
7. **ܩܫܝܫܐ qašīšē means "elders" or "priests", not "guards"** (L4 s.2, L7 s.1 and s.5, exercises; 9 occurrences). Use ܢܛܘܪܐ nāṭōrē, which is already in L11's vocabulary.
8. **Object pronouns.** L11 calls *qṭal leh* "standard, highly common" for "he killed him". In fact the verbal suffix is the normal form, and l- + pronoun is the marked one. The 3rd-plural object is the independent form ܐܢܘܢ ʾennōn / ܐܢܝܢ ʾennēn. Forms like *qṭalhūn* or *šbaqhūn* are not classical. These forms are drilled in L11 Ex. 1–2, in Appendix A.6 and in the L12 warm-up.
9. **Imperative versus perfect + suffix (L11 s.3).** The story glosses *parqanī* and *ʾassyanī* as imperatives, "save me! / heal me!", but they are perfects meaning "he saved me". The imperatives with suffix are pruqayn(y) and ʾassayn(y). The L11 Ex. 5 key and L12 warm-up Q2 ("Save me! (Imperative)") repeat the error.
10. **Paael and Ethpaal vowels.** The Paael 1 c.sg. imperfect is ʾqaddeš ܐܩܕܫ, not *ʾaqaddeš* (L6 s.8, L8 §4). The Ethpaal participle is metqaddaš, not *metqaddeš* (L6 s.9 and the "Looking ahead" note).

## 2. Lesson by lesson

**L0**

- In the practice-table analysis of nūhrā, final ܐ is not marked (NJ).

**L1**

- *ʾīt* is written without ʾ throughout L2 and L4 ("īt").

**L3**

## 3. Coherence

- **Four house styles.** L0 has its own label block. L1–4, 7, 8, 11 and 12 are compact. L5, 6, 9 and 10 are about twice as long, carry Word-era formatting and have a different section numbering (their answer key is in §8, not §9). L9 and L10 also put their stories in tables.
- **Inconsistent conventions:**
  - transliteration: ḇ/p̄ marks, ə schwas, malk-y vs malkī, hennōn vs ʾennūn
  - vocabulary counts: 30, 18, 15, 20, 28, 8
  - vocabulary repeated across lessons: rēšā, ʾen, tūb, rugzā/rūgzā
- **Sequencing:** forms are used before they are taught.
  - hollow qām from L3
  - the Ethpeel ʾetragzū and the imperfect in L5–6
  - verbal suffixes in L5
- **The SEDRA gate checks only that a surface form exists.** ܐܓܠܐ and ܝܠܦ both pass because they exist as other forms. So does every wrong gloss above. All 396 TSV forms return HTTP 200 today, so "100% SEDRA-verified" says nothing about whether the form fits its slot. A stronger gate would compare SEDRA's stem code and gloss with the TSV's stem and English columns.

## 4. Markdown or HTML?

Keep Markdown as the source. The tables broke because Syriac does not survive fixed-width (multiline and grid) tables and the converter cut cells mechanically. Markdown itself was not the problem.
- Pipe tables with one-line cells are robust.
- Pandoc already produces the HTML.
- Raw `<div class="sentence">` blocks (L0) give HTML-level control for interlinear text and still reach the docx output.

Hand-written HTML sources would be harder for you and agents to edit and diff. They would also lose the clean docx path.
