## 1. Errors in Syriac forms

### High: wrong form taught or used in a story

1. **L5, L6, L7: ܐܓܠܝ *ʾaglī* "he revealed"** [S]. SEDRA returns 404 for ܐܓܠܝ, so the form fails the project's own SEDRA gate. "Reveal" is the Peal ܓܠܐ *glā*, which is what L10 section 6 teaches (*glā* / *ʾetglī*). The Aphel of this root normally means "carry into exile" [G]. Affects L5 sections 2, 3, 6, the story (s.4), Ex. 1, 3, 4 and their keys, the L6 and L7 warm-ups. *Conflicts with the 2026-10-21 settled decision.*
2. **L9, L10: the imperative of ܐܙܠ is ܙܠ *zel*, plural ܙܠܘ** [S]. The lessons give *ʾezal* ܐܙܠ / *ʾezalw* ܐܙܠܘ, which are perfects. Affects L9 section 2.7, story s.4, Ex. 1 no. 1 and key, Ex. 3 no. 1, and the L10 warm-up Q1.
3. **L9: ܐܚܘܐ is "I will show", not "Show!"** [S]. SEDRA parses ܐܚܘܐ as 1 c.sg. imperfect Paael. The imperative is Paael ܚܘܐ *ḥawwā*; "show me" is ܚܘܢܝ *ḥawwān(y)*. The verb is a Paael, so it does not belong among the Aphel imperatives in section 2.4. Also section 2.7 ("Show me a sign").
4. **L7 s.5 and L8 warm-up Q4: ܐܝܬܝܘܗܝ is "they brought him" (perfect)** [S]. The imperative "bring him!" (m.pl.) is ܐܝܬܐܘܗܝ *ʾaytaʾū(h)y*. The L8 key explanation (*ʾaytīw* + *-hy*) describes the perfect.
5. **L11: ܐܣܐܝܢܝ *ʾassaynī* "heal me!"** [S: 404] [G]. A III-Yodh Paael imperative ends in *-ā* and takes the suffix directly: ܐܣܢܝ *ʾassān(y)*, like ܚܘܢܝ above. ܦܪܘܩܝܢܝ is right [S]. Affects section 4 note, story s.3, Ex. 5 key. *Conflicts with the 2026-10-21 settled decision*, which fixed both imperatives as *-aynī*.
6. **L10 section 3.3: Ethpeel paradigm** [S for the pattern: ܐܬܟܬܒܬ *ʾetkatbat*]. Before vocalic endings the vowel moves: 3 f.sg. *ʾetqaṭlat*, 1 c.sg. *ʾetqaṭlet*, participle *metqaṭlā*, *metqaṭlīn*, *metqaṭlān*. The lesson prints *ʾetqṭlat*, *ʾetqṭlet*, *metqṭlā*, *metqṭlīn*, *metqṭlān*. Its own Ex. 4 key has the right pattern (*ʾeštamʿat*).
7. **L5 s.7: text and transliteration disagree.** The Syriac reads ܘܐܬܕܡܪ ("and marvelled"); the transliteration, gloss and translation read *w-ḥdī* "and rejoiced".
8. **L5 s.5: *ʾaqrebtūn l-ʾAlāhā* translated "you have drawn near to God".** That is the Peal meaning (*qrebtūn*). The Aphel is transitive ("you brought near, offered"), which is the point of the lesson, and the mistranslation is repeated in the Ex. 5 key.

## 2. Grammar taught inaccurately or inconsistently

1. **Predicate adjectives (L1, L4, L5, L12).** L1 teaches *ʾAlāhā ṭābā*, *barnāšā bīšā hū*, *Yūḥannan hū ṭābā* with the emphatic adjective. In Syriac a predicate adjective stands in the absolute: *ṭāb*, *bīš*, *rabb* [G]. The course itself is inconsistent: L1 s.2 uses and names the feminine absolute (*rabbā hī*); L4 Ex. 3 uses the feminine emphatic (*ṭābtā hī*); L7 s.4 and L10 use the masculine absolute (*rgīz*, *gabrā qṭīl*, *brīk hū*); L12 Ex. 2 has *ṭābīn hennōn* next to *ṭābā hū* and *bīštā hī*. The rule is never stated. This is the single most pervasive problem, since it is in the first sentences a student learns.
2. **Silent endings are treated three different ways.**
   - Perfect 3 m.pl.: *ktabū*, *ʾemarū*, *ʾeštaddarū* (L3 to L8, L10 keys) but *šarrīw*, *ʾesarw*, *dḥelw* (L9 and L10 stories). In classical pronunciation the waw is silent [G]; L3 says so only in a parenthesis.
   - Imperative m.pl.: *ktūb(w)* in L9, *qṭolū* in Appendix A.3.
   - 1 c.sg. suffix: *malk-y*, *ʾab-y* (L4) with no statement that the yodh is silent; *qaṭlanī*, *pruqaynī* (L11) but *šmaʿayn(y)* (L9 key). "He killed me" and "he killed us" are homophones (*qaṭlan*), which L11 hides.
3. **L8 section 3 contradicts its own table.** The text assigns the *t-* prefix to "they (f.)"; the table correctly has *neqṭlān*.
4. **Theme vowel notation.** *neqṭūl (neqṭol)* in L8; *nerdop*, *neʿroq*, *neknoš* beside *neqṭūl* in one L9 table; *qṭol* in Appendix A beside *teqṭūl* in the same paragraph. Likewise *u*/*ū* before a closed syllable: *purqānā*, *šulṭānā*, *kurhānā* but *šūbḥā*, *qūrbānā*, *šūddārā*, *būrktā*, *gūbbā*.
5. **Other transliteration drift.** *ḵ* is marked only in L4, L11 and a few words (*lāḵ*, *ʾaḵḥdā*, *ḥešūḵā*), nowhere else is spirantization shown; *-ēky* (L4) vs *-ēḵ(y)* (L11, Appendix); *ʾanti* (L1) vs *ʾantī* (Appendix); *lwāt* (L7) vs *l-wāt* (L11); *ʾarbaʿā* (L12) vs *ʾarbʿā* (Appendix); *īdaʿ*, *īled*, *īḥīdāyā*, *īdaʿtā* without the initial *ʾ* that every other vowel-initial word carries; *yahab* (L6 s.7) vs *yahb / yhab* (L7); "Ethpeel" with *th* beside "Etpaael" without.
6. **L11: 1 c.sg. "Nun buffer".** The *n* is part of the suffix (*-an(y)*), not a buffer inserted to avoid clumsiness.
7. **L11 Ex. 3 no. 2 calls prolepsis "extreme emphasis";** the summary in the same lesson calls it "clarity or emphasis". It is ordinary Syriac style.
8. **L12 s.3: *ʾen ʾaynā barnāšā*.** *ʾaynā* is "which?"; "if anyone" is *ʾen ʾnāš* [G].
9. **Overstatements.** L3: the verb "will almost always come first"; L12: Syriac "prefers" the demonstrative after the noun. Both orders are common in each case. L1: Syriac as a literary language "from the 2nd to the 8th centuries" (it runs to the 13th at least).
10. **L1 s.1: *malkā hū* is glossed "He is a king. / There is a king."** The second needs *ʾīt*, taught in L4.
11. **L5 and L6: *rūḥā* is called feminine and takes feminine verbs in L5 s.11, but masculine verbs in L5 Ex. 3 no. 4, Ex. 4 key no. 3 and the L6 warm-up.**
12. **Comparative notes.** Arabic *maqṭūl* (L10) should be *maqtūl*. L3 table: Arabic and Hebrew 2 f.pl. and Hebrew 3 f.pl. are shown as "-", though the forms exist. L11: *auton apekteinan* is plural ("they killed him").
13. **L0.** ʿE is described as a "pharyngeal stop" (it is a fricative). "All letters are consonants" is followed by *nūhrā* and *Yūḥannan*, where Waw is a vowel letter, with no explanation. The note that Taw "often appears to connect" confuses a letter the same section lists as a non-joiner.

## 3. Coherence: what the course promises and does not deliver

1. **Vowel signs are never taught.** L0 says vowels are "to be covered in Lesson 1". L1 does not cover them, and no lesson does. All Syriac is unpointed, so students depend entirely on the transliteration.
2. **Plural nouns are never taught, and no plural carries *syāmē*.** There is not one U+0308 in the thirteen lessons. ܬܠܡܝܕܐ, ܢܛܘܪܐ, ܟܗܢܐ, ܡܠܐ are printed identically for singular and plural from L2 on. Only Appendix A.2 mentions the plural endings and *syāmē*. Related gaps: suffixes on plural nouns (*talmīdāw-hy* from L3, *mellāw-hy*, *ʾīdāw-hy*) and the absolute plural (*gabrīn* in the L12 key) are used but not taught.
3. **Hollow verbs are never taught.** L3 introduces *qām* as "preview of L7"; L7 covers I-Alaph, I-Yodh and III-Yodh only. *qām*, *dān*, *ṣām*, *lāṭ*, *ʾarīm*, *ʾaqīm*, *tāb* are used throughout, and L5 Ex. 2 asks for a full conjugation of *ʾarīm* "using the paradigm as a model", which cannot produce the key's answer (*mrīm*). I-Nun is explained only in an L5 preview and the appendix.
4. **The past continuous (participle + *hwā*) appears only in Appendix A.5.**
5. **Sequencing.** Forms are used before they are taught, mostly without a note: *b-* and *w-* (L1 story; L1 section 3 introduces only *d-* and *l-*); feminine adjectives (*ṭābtā*, L2 key); I-Alaph and III-Yodh perfects (L3, L5); *ʾītawhy* (L4); Aphel imperative with suffix (L7); suffixed imperative (L9 key). L5 to L10 mark a pronoun object with *l-* (*šaddar lī*); L11 then says the suffix is the normal form. L11 section 1 should say that the earlier usage was a simplification.
6. **Story continuity.**
   - L4 ends at the gate; L5 and L6 have Yukhannan preaching to crowds and in a temple with priests; L7 resumes with the guards reporting "what happened at the gate". L5 and L6 read as a separate story.
   - L9 says the king has heard of "miracles" and orders the soldiers not to fear "his signs"; none has been narrated.
   - L4 s.8: asked what is in his hand, Yukhannan answers that he has no "evil garment".
   - L8 s.3: the king says he judges "that I might do evil to you".
   - L11 s.6: *ʾAlāhā nnīḥāḵ* is the usual wish for the dead ("God give you rest") [?].
   - L8 Ex. 4 no. 3 ("If you do not leave heaven, you will see God") and L7 Ex. 3 no. 3 ("the king knew wrath") make little sense.
7. **Structure.**
   - L6 and L9 have no Section 8 (numbering jumps 7 to 9). L9 and L10 have no Section 11; L10 hides five unrelated "Additional words" (widowhood, grass) inside section 6.
   - L9 and L10 put warm-up answers in the question table; the others use the answer key.
   - L0 uses a different heading block and sentence markup from L1 to L12.
   - L2 section 4 footnotes refer to asterisks that are not in the table. L5 section 10 has a stray asterisk (`*-t(y)**`). L9 section 3.1 has "Always" capitalized mid-sentence.
   - L11 s.5 carries a note about *qrāyhy*, a form that is not in the sentence.
8. **Vocabulary.**
   - *Counts.* L10 section 6 is headed "30 Words" and has 32 rows.
   - *Duplicates.* 26 words appear in the vocabulary tables of more than one lesson, against the 2026-10-06 dedupe decision. ܕܝܢܐ is listed four times (L1, L5, L6, L8); ܚܝܐ and ܥܠܡܐ three times each. L11 section 11 calls its list "genuinely new" but repeats ܣܗܕ and ܢܛܘܪܐ. L12 section 11 presents ܠܐ as new vocabulary.
   - *"High-frequency".* The Section 11 lists in L1 to L4, L7 and L8 are described as high-frequency but contain words such as bone, snow, winepress, scab, nakedness, paternal aunt, none of which occurs in any story or exercise. Meanwhile words the stories need (*ʿammā*, *gabrā*, *māryā*, *kull*, *meddem*, *rāzā*, *lebbā*, *šmayyā*, *mawtā*) head no row of any lesson's vocabulary table (checked by grep; *ʿammā*, *kull* and *rāzā* are in the TSV, the other six are not).
   - *Layout.* L2 section 6 and L3 section 6 pair unrelated verbs and particles in the same row; *talmīdā* sits under "Adverb/Particle".
   - *Synthetic forms.* L9 section 3.3 and Ex. 2 generate Paael and Aphel infinitives mechanically for roots that have no such stem (*mkattābū*, *mšammāʿū*, *mašdārū*, *masgādū*), which sits badly with the attested-forms-only policy.

## 4. Suggested order of work

1. Decide 1.1 and 1.5 (they reopen settled decisions), then fix section 1 "High" in the lessons, the checks JSON and the tests.
2. Settle one rule each for predicate adjectives (2.1) and silent endings (2.2), and apply it everywhere. These two change more lines than everything else together.
3. Add the missing teaching: plurals with *syāmē* (L1 or L2), hollow and I-Nun verbs (L7), participle + *hwā* (L7 or L8), and either a vowel-sign section or a removal of the promise in L0.
4. Fix section 1 "Medium" and the glosses.
5. Structure, duplicates and story continuity.

A stronger gate would have caught most of section 1: instead of testing that a spelling exists, compare SEDRA's parse (tense, stem, person, suffix) and gloss with what the lesson claims for the form.
