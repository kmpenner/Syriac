This is a highly effective, immersive way to learn. By mapping a continuous narrative to the grammar, your group will build high-context reading endurance from day one. Since your goal is early Christian literature, we will theme the continuing story as an original "Apocryphal Act" — let's call it The Acts of Mar Yukhannan (an ascetic monk traveling to a foreign kingdom to confront a local ruler and perform miracles).

Here is the 12-lesson frequency-first blueprint, specifically tailored to your group's existing knowledge of Semitic and classical languages.

Course Outline: The Acts of Mar Yukhannan

Lesson 1: The Core Architecture

 \* Morphology: The Alphabet, Emphatic State (the default noun form), and Independent Pronouns.

 \* Syntax: Verbless nominal clauses, the d- particle (relative and possessive), and the l- preposition (direct objects and "to/for").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 high-frequency nouns (God, man, earth, light, word), pronouns, and primary prepositions.

 \* The Story (Part 1): The setting. A description of the king, the kingdom, and Mar Yukhannan. (Written entirely in verbless sentences, using d- and l- to establish relationships).

Lesson 2: The Narrative Present

 \* Morphology: The Peal Active Participle and Enclitic (attached) Pronouns.

 \* Syntax: Forming the present tense by fusing participles and enclitics (e.g., kaneb-na, "I am writing").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 common strong roots (write, go, speak, hear, see) learned in their participle forms, plus basic adverbs.

 \* The Story (Part 2): Yukhannan is speaking to his disciples and preparing for his journey.

Lesson 3: The Narrative Past

 \* Morphology: The Peal Perfect (Strong Verbs).

 \* Syntax: Standard Syriac word order (Verb-Subject-Object) and the use of the conjunction w- (and) to chain past actions.

 \* Vocabulary: 30 new strong verbs and common narrative transitions (hašo \- now, den \- but/then).

 \* The Story (Part 3): Yukhannan travels to the foreign kingdom and stands before the city gates.

Lesson 4: Personalizing the Text

 \* Morphology: Pronominal Suffixes attached to nouns (my, your, his/her) and prepositions.

 \* Syntax: Expressing possession without the d- particle, and the idiomatic use of l- \+ suffix to mean "to have" (e.g., "there is to me").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 terms for family members, body parts, and items of clothing.

 \* The Story (Part 4): A dialogue at the gate. The guards ask about Yukhannan's origins, his family, and what he possesses.

Lesson 5: The Aphel Stem (Divine Action)

 \* Morphology: The Aphel Perfect and Participle (Causative stem).

 \* Syntax: Causative action, heavily used in theology (e.g., "to cause to live/save," "to bring near").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 verbs common to divine or royal action (save, destroy, reveal, send) and abstract theological nouns.

 \* The Story (Part 5): Yukhannan preaches to the crowds, explaining what God has caused to happen in the world.

Lesson 6: The Paael Stem (Intensive Action)

 \* Morphology: The Paael Perfect and Participle (Intensive/Factitive stem).

 \* Syntax: Actions that are repeated, intensified, or done to multiple objects.

 \* Vocabulary: 30 Paael-heavy verbs (bless, receive, speak/converse, teach) and temple/worship terminology.

 \* The Story (Part 6): Yukhannan blesses the people and teaches them a new way to worship, angering the local priests.

Lesson 7: Navigating Weak Roots

 \* Morphology: The Peal Perfect of I-Alaph, I-Yodh, and III-Yodh verbs.

 \* Syntax: How phonetic shifts impact the spelling of highly common verbs without changing the underlying sentence structure.

 \* Vocabulary: 30 of the most irregular but unavoidable verbs (to say, to know, to give, to come, to be).

 \* The Story (Part 7): The local priests go to the king and tell him what Yukhannan has done. The king summons him.

Lesson 8: The Subjunctive Future

 \* Morphology: The Imperfect Tense (across Peal, Paael, and Aphel).

 \* Syntax: Using the Imperfect to express the future, and using d- \+ Imperfect to express purpose or subjunctive ideas ("in order that he might...").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 words related to time, judgment, future events, and royal court terminology.

 \* The Story (Part 8): Yukhannan stands before the king. The king threatens what he will do to Yukhannan if he does not leave.

Lesson 9: Commands and Intentions

 \* Morphology: Imperatives and Infinitives.

 \* Syntax: Direct commands, and using the preposition l- \+ Infinitive to complete the action of a main verb (e.g., "he began to speak").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 military, emotional, and action-oriented terms (strike, fear, rejoice, bind).

 \* The Story (Part 9): The king commands his soldiers to bind Yukhannan and throw him into a furnace.

Lesson 10: The Passive Voice

 \* Morphology: The Ethpaal and Ettaphal Stems.

 \* Syntax: Expressing passive and reflexive actions.

 \* Vocabulary: 30 verbs that appear frequently in the passive (to be baptized, to be revealed, to be saved, to be killed).

 \* The Story (Part 10): Yukhannan is thrown into the fire, but he is saved by a divine occurrence. The miracle is revealed to the city.

Lesson 11: Verbal Suffixes (The Fast Direct Object)

 \* Morphology: Pronominal suffixes attached directly to verbs.

 \* Syntax: Bypassing the l- preposition to attach the direct object directly to the verb (e.g., "he-saved-me").

 \* Vocabulary: 30 words focused on healing, nature, and community.

 \* The Story (Part 11): The king is struck with an illness. He begs Yukhannan, saying, "Heal me, save me, forgive me."

Lesson 12: The Fringes of Syntax

 \* Morphology: Demonstrative Pronouns (this, that) and Numbers.

 \* Syntax: Conditional clauses (en \- "if") and complex comparative sentences.

 \* Vocabulary: Numbers 1-10, demonstratives, and philosophical/concluding terminology (truth, eternity, peace).

 \* The Story (Part 12): Yukhannan heals the king. The king establishes a new decree based on conditions ("If any man..."). Yukhannan departs in peace.
