# Instructor Notes: Introduction to Classical Syriac

These notes go with *The Acts of Mar Yukhannan*, Lessons 0–12, taught in one 13-week term (see `Schedule.tex`). Each week has three parts: students do the interactive lesson before class; class time goes to grammar, the story and the exercises; homework is the written exercises plus a translation.

## General pacing

- **A typical week (about 2.5 contact hours):** 20 min to review the warm-up and last week's homework, 40 min for the new grammar, 45 min to read the story aloud and translate it together, and 25 min to start the exercises.
- **Read the story aloud every week,** first the instructor and then the students in turn. Reading fluency in the script lags behind grammar unless it is practised in class.
- **Vocabulary:** about 30 words a week. Students should use the flashcard deck daily rather than cram, and the weekly quiz keeps them honest.
- **Use the Module tests (Feb 2, Mar 9) to diagnose.** If most of the class misses the perfect or the suffixes in Module 1, spend 30 minutes of Week 5 on them before the Aphel.
- **Expect Weeks 5–8 to be the crunch.** Three new verb systems (Aphel, Paael, weak roots) and then the imperfect arrive in quick succession. Lesson 8 (imperfect) is the hardest single lesson. If you must slip, let Lesson 12 slip into the review week rather than compress Lesson 8.

## Lesson by lesson

### Lesson 0: The Alphabet (Week 1, first half)
- **Pacing:** one class for the letters, then a short dictation. Students should write every letter by hand, not just recognise it.
- **Discussion:** Why did Syriac Christians develop three scripts (Estrangela, Serto, East Syriac)? What does the script tell you about where a manuscript came from?
- **Common errors:**
  - confusing ܕ Dalath and ܪ Resh (only the dot position differs);
  - mixing up the three s-sounds ܣ s, ܫ š and ܨ ṣ;
  - joining the non-joining letters (ܐ ܕ ܗ ܘ ܙ ܨ ܪ ܬ) to the next letter;
  - reading final ܐ as a consonant.

### Lesson 1: Core Architecture (Week 1, second half)
- **Pacing:** the emphatic state and pronouns are quick. Spend time on verbless clauses and on *d-*, because they recur constantly.
- **Discussion:** English needs "is" and Syriac does not. What does the emphatic state do that English "the" does not, given that *malkā* can mean "a king" or "the king"?
- **Common errors:**
  - translating every emphatic noun as definite;
  - reading *d-* only as "of" (it is also the relative "who/which");
  - confusing *l-* "to/for" with *l-* as the object marker.

### Lesson 2: The Narrative Present (Week 2)
- **Pacing:** drill the participle with enclitics orally, for example *kāteb-nā, kātbā-nā, kātbīn-ḥnan*.
- **Discussion:** Why does a narrative use participles at all? Compare the historic present in Greek and in English storytelling.
- **Common errors:**
  - reading the participle as a past tense;
  - forgetting that the participle agrees in gender and number;
  - mistaking *kāteb* (participle) for *ktab* (perfect) in unvocalised text.

### Lesson 3: The Narrative Past (Week 3)
- **Pacing:** the perfect paradigm must be memorised this week. Run a 5-minute paradigm chant at the start of every class until Week 6.
- **Discussion:** How does *w-* plus the perfect drive a narrative? Compare *wayyiqtol* in Hebrew, if students know it.
- **Common errors:**
  - ܟܬܒܬ is three forms (*ketbat* "she", *ktabt* "you", *ketbet* "I") with the same consonants. Insist on reading for context.
  - Expecting subject–verb order.

### Lesson 4: Personalizing the Text (Week 4)
- **Pacing:** suffixes on nouns, then on prepositions. The irregular nouns (*ʾab, ʾaḥ, bar, brattā, baytā*) need their own drill.
- **Discussion:** The story's family vocabulary. How do kinship terms work as metaphors in early Christian texts ("Father", "brothers")?
- **Common errors:**
  - *-eh* "his" vs *-āh* "her", which look alike unvocalised (ܡܠܟܗ);
  - adding the suffix to the emphatic form (*malkā-y* for *malk-ī*);
  - forgetting the *d-* + *dīl-* alternative.

### Lesson 5: The Aphel Stem (Week 5, after the Module 1 test)
- **Pacing:** introduce the idea of derived stems through the overview table in Appendix A.4 before the Aphel itself.
- **Discussion:** Causation in theology: *ʾaḥyī* "he made alive, saved". What does it mean that salvation vocabulary is causative?
- **Common errors:**
  - reading the *ʾa-* prefix as a 1 c.sg. imperfect (*ʾe-*);
  - missing the assimilated Nun in *ʾappeq*;
  - treating *ʾallep* (Aphel of ʾ-l-p) as a Paael.

### Lesson 6: The Paael Stem (Week 6)
- **Pacing:** the doubling is invisible in unvocalised script, so teach students to suspect a Paael from the meaning and the vocabulary list.
- **Discussion:** Liturgical vocabulary is overwhelmingly Paael (*qaddeš, barrek, šabbaḥ, ṣallī, šammeš*). Why might that be?
- **Common errors:**
  - reading *qaddeš* as Peal *qdeš*;
  - forgetting the *m-* prefix on the Paael and Aphel participles;
  - confusing *mbarrek* (participle) with *barrek* (perfect).

### Lesson 7: Weak Roots (Week 7, after the study break)
- **Pacing:** start with a short review of the perfect, because the break erodes it. Then take one weak class per day: III-Yodh, then I-Alaph and I-Yodh, then I-Nun and ʾetā.
- **Discussion:** How do you look up a weak verb in a lexicon? Practise finding *ḥzā, ʾetā, īdaʿ* in SEDRA by root.
- **Common errors:**
  - looking for three consonants in *ḥzā* or *ʾetā*;
  - reading *ʾemar* as an Aphel because it starts with Alaph;
  - mixing up *ḥzāt* "she saw" and *ḥzayt* "you saw".

### Lesson 8: The Imperfect (Week 8)
- **Pacing:** the hardest lesson. Spend a full class on the paradigm alone. Note that 3 m.sg. and 1 c.pl. are identical (*neqṭūl*), and so are 3 f.sg. and 2 m.sg. (*teqṭūl*).
- **Discussion:** Future, purpose (*d-* + imperfect) and wish all use one form. How does a reader decide which is meant?
- **Common errors:**
  - reading the prefix *n-* as a root Nun;
  - forgetting that I-Alaph verbs contract (*nēmar*, not *neʾmar*);
  - translating every imperfect as a future.

### Lesson 9: Commands and Intentions (Week 9, after the Module 2 test)
- **Pacing:** the imperative builds on the imperfect stem, so revise *teqṭūl → qṭol*. The infinitive with *l-* comes quickly once *meqṭal* is learned.
- **Discussion:** The soldier who says "We are not able to kill a righteous man." Use it to discuss martyr acts as a genre and the figure of the hesitant executioner.
- **Common errors:**
  - negating the imperative (*lā qṭol*) instead of using *lā teqṭūl*;
  - reading *meqṭal* as a participle;
  - mixing up *ʾezlaw* "go!" (imperative) and *ʾezalw* "they went".

### Lesson 10: The Passive Voice (Week 10)
- **Pacing:** use the stem-and-partner table (Appendix A.4) as the anchor. Leave metathesis (*ʾeštmaʿ, ʾeštaddar*) for the second class.
- **Discussion:** The fiery furnace and Daniel 3. How do apocryphal Acts reuse biblical type-scenes?
- **Common errors:**
  - reading the *ʾet-* prefix as part of the root;
  - missing the metathesis before sibilants;
  - confusing *ʾetqṭel* (Ethpeel perfect) with *metqṭel* (participle).

### Lesson 11: Verbal Suffixes (Week 11; no class on Good Friday)
- **Pacing:** one class only in a short week. Focus on 3rd-person suffixes and *-anī*, and treat the 2nd-person forms as recognition only.
- **Discussion:** "Heal me, save me": the king's plea. How do Syriac healing stories link bodily and spiritual healing?
- **Common errors:**
  - reading *qaṭlanī* "he killed me" as *qṭaln* "we killed";
  - missing the vowel shift *qṭal → qaṭl-*;
  - reading the double marking (suffix + *l-*) as two objects.

### Lesson 12: The Fringes of Syntax (Week 12)
- **Pacing:** demonstratives and comparison are easy. Numbers with reverse gender agreement need a drill.
- **Discussion:** The ending, with forgiveness instead of punishment. How does this story's theology compare with the Acts of Thomas?
- **Common errors:**
  - putting *tlāt* with masculine nouns;
  - translating comparative *men* as "from";
  - mixing up *hāy* "that (f.)" and *hī* "she".

### Week 13: Review
- Read a short Peshitta passage the students have not seen; Mark 1:1–8 is a good fit for this vocabulary. Show them how much they can already do with a lexicon.
- Go over the Final Exam review guide.
